II. Product Background
TK Sport is the client of this project. Its Zalo group has about 100 members and the venue regularly serves about 20–30 participants per day. Players include regular customers and occasional visitors. Social sessions run Monday to Sunday in three slots: 16:00–18:00, 18:00–20:00 and 20:00–22:00. Each slot uses two courts and two referees. Each court accepts up to eight people and may serve a different skill group. The website supports badminton and pickleball with the same social workflow. [S1, S5]
Lâm, the venue manager, uses Zalo to organize the sessions in order to avoid Alobo's subscription fee, while also allowing him and the organizers to communicate directly with participants and keep track of them more closely. The group has a public invitation link, and the organizers create polls for players to register for different time slots. After registering, players contact the organizer through private Zalo messages to arrange payment. Before each session, the organizer has to compare the poll results with payment messages and also rely on personal knowledge of the players, such as knowing who the regular customers are. According to Lâm, this is a fragmented process because there is no central place to track player information, registration status, and payment status. [S1]
At present, players of similar skill levels are arranged on different days and are commonly grouped using labels such as beginner, six months of experience, one year of experience, or more. Referees or coordinators observe each player's ability and remember the regular players, but these assessments are not stored in a centralized player profile. As a result, placing players into suitable groups depends heavily on the memory and availability of the person in charge. It is especially difficult to verify the skill level of a player who only self-declares their ability compared with someone who has already played at the venue before. [S1]
The venue has experienced cases where players registered for groups above their actual skill level. Some players intentionally chose stronger groups because they wanted to gain more experience. Players with noticeably different skill levels have complained that these mismatches reduce the quality of the sessions they paid to join. Therefore, the problem is not that the referees do not understand the players' skill levels, but that there is no consistent mechanism for recording, reviewing, and applying these assessments before and during registration. [S1]
TK Sport's first priority is to reduce skill mismatches. The second is to reduce manual player management, registration and payment checks. Lâm and the referee team will use the website in a one-to-two-week trial and report their experience. The team will record actual complaints, processing work and feedback. Customer retention remains a longer-term objective. [S1]

IV. Proposed Solution
TK Sport Community will manage badminton and pickleball social sessions at TK Sport. Referees record observed skills in a profile for each sport. The website uses the published result for suitable-session suggestions and both lower and upper skill checks. New players receive a provisional survey result, followed by on-court observation. Lâm decides disputed-rating corrections through the Manager account after the referee team reviews the evidence. System Admin has no authority to make this decision.
The website links session availability, individual places, friend invitations and verified bank transfers. Friends must accept before payment for their places. A Social Booking Order contains only participation charges, including the assessment already covered by the ticket. A published rating change affects only future tickets that become ineligible; the original payer receives the relevant refund. Other valid tickets and separate goods orders remain unchanged.
Members can view community profiles, add friends, send messages and request partners. Contact details, payment records and internal assessment notes are not public. Referees continue to assign games and rotate players. Staff handles counter sales, session supplies and reconciliation through separate records. Manager reviews business exceptions, while Owner approves policies and prices and views business summaries.
The roles separate day-to-day venue work from technical support. Trần Minh Đức supports Lâm through TK.admin during initial operation; Lâm uses TK.manager. Each account has one role. A later technical handover keeps Lâm's Manager account separate and preserves the former operator's history. The trial covers the social workflow and the approved sales, stock and control tasks. The badminton rubric and timetable in the appendices remain proposed pilot settings. [S5]

Appendix A. Proposed Badminton Skill Framework
Status: proposed pilot configuration for Lâm and the referee team to review. Direct referee assessment is confirmed; the bands, rubric and review triggers below are recommendations, not existing TK Sport rules. Broad technical, tactical and movement categories draw on BWF Coach Education Level 1; the project-designed score is not a BWF rating or a pickleball scale. [S4]
A1. Five Clear Bands and a 0–100 Internal Score
Use five visible bands, B1–B5, rather than years of playing. Score five dimensions from 0 to 4 and multiply their sum by five. Equal weights make the initial rubric easy to apply; the result is an internal assessment whose usefulness depends on consistent observation and calibration.
Band / Score
Observable playing profile
Next development focus
B1 — Beginner0–19
Needs frequent guidance on basic contacts, service, court positioning and continuing a short rally.
Develop a repeatable serve, controlled contact and a simple return-to-position habit.
B2 — Foundation20–39
Can join basic social games but is inconsistent across shot types, movement and rallies.
Improve routine serve/return, basic clear and net/lift control, and partner communication.
B3 — Developing40–59
Sustains moderate-paced play and uses several shots with emerging tactical and doubles awareness.
Reduce avoidable errors, recover after shots and coordinate attacking/defensive positions.
B4 — Intermediate60–79
Handles varied recreational rallies with more consistent technique, movement and partnership decisions.
Improve decision quality under pressure, coverage of weaker areas and controlled changes of pace.
B5 — Advanced recreational80–100
Maintains broad skill coverage, reliable positioning and partner coordination at stronger social pace.
Refine tactical variation and consistent performance across partners; no professional status is implied.
Boundaries do not overlap: 20 belongs to B2, 40 to B3, 60 to B4 and 80 to B5. Eligibility uses the displayed, published score and band, with no hidden rounding rule. A referee may approve a session-specific exception. Publish band changes through the review process below, not after one rally or an unusually good game.

Leave the dimension unscored; do not treat lack of observation as zero. A full numerical assessment requires evidence for all five dimensions.
Assessment score = 5 × (D1 + D2 + D3 + D4 + D5). Example: marks of 2, 2, 2, 3 and 2 produce 55 points, placing the player in B3. Referees should record at least one concise piece of evidence for any unusually high/low mark and for a proposed band change. The assessment record includes player, court session, referee, rubric version, observations and publication status.
Record wins, losses, partner/opponent identities and the number of observed games as contextual evidence where available. Raw win rate must not automatically generate skill points: a player may win more often because of a stronger partner or weaker opponents. For the initial pilot, persistent results that seem inconsistent with a band trigger referee review rather than an automatic promotion. This retains match performance as assessment evidence while recognising that a small, unadjusted sample is insufficient for a fair individual ranking.
A3. Immediate Onboarding and the First Two Sessions
The questionnaire assigns a provisional band immediately; it does not certify ability. Self-reporting remains a possible source of initial mismatch, so the referee must see who is provisional and prioritise observation. The one-to-two-session target is an initial review window, not a guarantee that every player can be assessed accurately within two visits.
Topic

Proposed mapping: score the five skill self-checks with the same 0–4 anchors and calculate a provisional 0–100 score; use the lower supported anchor for an uncertain answer. Background and frequency do not add points. Publish “Provisional — awaiting on-court assessment”, allow registration to a matching published session, and flag inconsistent answers for priority observation rather than an unexplained rejection.
During the first attended session, the assigned referee observes several rallies with normal partner rotation and records the skills actually seen. During the second session, missing evidence is completed and another referee may contribute where practical. Replace the questionnaire result with the supported on-court assessment rather than keeping self-declared marks as a permanent component. If evidence remains insufficient, keep the provisional status and state that further observation is needed; unobserved dimensions must remain unscored.
A4. Progression, Review and Assessor Consistency
More frequent play creates more opportunities to demonstrate progress; it does not automatically increase skill points. Keep attendance or customer-loyalty information separate from ability. Each player’s development path combines the current band, the general next-band expectations and a dated review history; detailed referee notes are visible only to Referees and the Manager as required.
Stage / Trigger

Publication safeguard
Initial placement
Survey creates a provisional band; complete on-court observations during the first one or two attended sessions where evidence permits.
Do not label the survey result referee-confirmed.
Routine review
After approximately four attended sessions since the last full assessment, or earlier when a referee identifies meaningful change, complete a fresh review.
The visit count triggers observation, not automatic promotion or mandatory score change.
Move to another band
Seek two separate observations supporting the destination band and confirmation by another approved referee where available.
Require the player to satisfy the destination band’s essential movement and doubles-coordination expectations; a strong smash alone cannot compensate for major gaps.

Escalate obvious misplacement or a complaint to Lâm and the referee team for prompt review.
The Manager records the corrected score, reason and evidence; preserve the earlier record and apply the affected-ticket policy.
Long absence
Flag the profile for renewed observation when the player returns.
Do not lower a score merely because the player has attended less frequently.
For ordinary reviews after confirmation, use the mean of the latest two complete on-court assessments as a simple supporting score, rounded to the nearest whole point. A band-crossing result remains a proposed change until the checks above are satisfied; the last published score remains active meanwhile. Lâm may correct a demonstrably wrong result immediately after a documented review. These are pilot rules to calibrate, not promises of statistical accuracy.
Before the pilot, both referees should independently score a small, consenting sample across the ability range, compare differences and refine ambiguous anchors. Keep the rubric version consistent and record changes. Report actual agreement and limitations rather than claiming a universally correct score.

B2. Why This Pattern Is Suitable for a Pilot
B1/B2 players receive regular early opportunities, while most B3/B4 sessions use the popular time windows. Tuesday and Thursday rotate beginner sessions into 18:00; Saturday offers B5 at 18:00. Separate court allocations give members choices without deliberately mixing incompatible bands.
The timetable represents 42 possible court sessions (7 × 3 × 2), or at most 336 participant places. This is capacity, not an attendance or revenue forecast. With about 20–30 participants per day, publish selectively and adjust the following week to observed interest and registrations.
B3. Publication and Registration Rules
Proposed activation rule: four to eight eligible participants per doubles social court. Before accepting payments, Lâm must approve the minimum-attendance deadline and cancellation/refund procedure. Where only one group has adequate demand, use the suitable court allocation rather than merging incompatible bands.
Friends must each meet the selected session’s band conditions, accept the invitation and have a verified payment. An invitation is not a confirmed ticket. Expired unpaid places are released individually; paid friends’ places remain valid. Hold durations must be configured before launch.
Recheck eligibility, capacity and time conflicts at confirmation. Exceptions identify the player and exact session. Never silently change the band of a sold session. After a rating change, recommend suitable available alternatives nearest the original date/time; do not automatically rebook or charge.
