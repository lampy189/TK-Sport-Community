I. Overall Requirements
1. Context Diagram
TK Sport Community (TKSC) is a web application that supports badminton and pickleball social sessions at TK Sport. It manages player profiles, sport-specific skill assessments, session registration, payments, community interactions, counter sales, stock and session supplies. Both sports share the same operational workflow while maintaining separate skill profiles and assessment records.
The context diagram represents TKSC as a single system and shows its information exchanges with seven human actors. Guest submits registration and sign-in information. Player exchanges profile and survey data, booking and payment requests, invitation responses, messages, and rating-review or skill-exception requests. The system returns published skill results, suitable sessions, tickets, request outcomes and notifications. Referee records attendance and assessments, decides session-specific skill exceptions, provides rating-review evidence, and requests or reports the use of session supplies.
Staff records counter sales, goods handover, stock movements, payment reviews, repayment evidence and shift reconciliation. Manager manages sessions, sport assessment schemes and operational assignments, approves Staff and Referee appointments, makes final disputed-rating decisions after Referee review, and reviews stock or financial exceptions within approved authority. Owner approves business policies, prices and Manager appointments, and receives business summaries and escalated cases. System Admin implements approved account access, maintains technical settings, handles processing incidents and records technical handover. This role does not make rating or business-approval decisions.
TKSC also exchanges data with five external services. Google OAuth provides verified sign-in identities. Bank / Payment Service supplies authenticated incoming transaction events and receives event acknowledgements. Cloudinary handles image storage and media operations. Firebase Service supports real-time messaging and push notifications. Email Service sends account verification, password reset, booking and refund-related emails and returns delivery status.
Each arrow indicates the direction of an information flow across the system boundary. Detailed permissions are specified in Section I.4.4. Social booking orders and goods sales orders remain separate, and incoming payment confirmation does not establish that an outgoing refund has been completed.

2. Main Business Flow
Each diagram shows the normal successful path. Declines, failed checks, late payments and other exceptions are specified in Section II. The shared workflow applies to both sports; sport-specific records are always retained.
2.1 Publish a court session (BF-01)
Trigger: Manager prepares a social schedule under Owner-approved policies.
End condition: A valid session is open for registration.

Figure 2. BF-01 — normal workflow.
Do not publish overlapping use of a court or referee. Do not silently change the sport, time, price or skill conditions of a sold session. UC-26–UC-28.
2.2 Join a suitable session (BF-02)
Trigger: A player selects a sport and date.
End condition: The player holds a confirmed ticket or receives a clear reason for failure.

Figure 3. BF-02 — normal workflow.
A full session, overlapping booking or out-of-band level blocks normal registration. Duplicate or late payment notices do not create extra tickets. UC-04, UC-06, UC-08, UC-11–UC-13, UC-32.
2.3 Register with friends (BF-03)
Trigger: A player chooses eligible friends for a session.
End condition: Each accepted, paid participant has their own ticket.

Figure 4. BF-03 — normal workflow.
An invitation is not a ticket. Unpaid expired places are released separately. An inviter's payment does not replace a friend's consent, skill check or time-conflict check. UC-09–UC-12.
2.4 Attend and receive a skill assessment (BF-04)
Trigger: A player arrives for a booked session.
End condition: Attendance and supported assessment results are stored.

Figure 5. BF-04 — normal workflow.
Unobserved skills are not scored as zero. Referees continue to allocate games and rotate players. A published rating change starts the future-ticket check in BF-05. UC-16–UC-18.
2.5 Review a rating and handle affected tickets (BF-05)
Trigger: Player requests a rating review, or a supported result is published. Manager decides disputed corrections; Referees provide evidence.
End condition: Only ineligible future paid tickets enter refunds; suitable alternatives are shown.

Figure 6. BF-05 — normal workflow.
Manager records the review decision, then the system checks affected future tickets. Staff handles eligible repayment and verified evidence. System Admin does not decide ratings or repayments. Preserve eligible friends’ tickets and separate Sales Orders; no automatic rebooking.
2.6 Connect with a playing partner (BF-06)
Trigger: A member views another member's profile or the participant list.
End condition: Members can communicate and record a partner preference.

Figure 7. BF-06 — normal workflow.
Friends can chat without a shared booking. A partner preference needs valid tickets and does not promise an exclusive pairing. Neither messages nor peer feedback directly change a skill rating. UC-23–UC-25.
2.7 Counter sale and goods handover (BF-07)
Staff creates a goods-only order. TKSC reserves available stock and issues a sales reference. The bank event confirms payment; Staff separately records physical handover. UC-36–UC-38.

Figure 8. Counter sale and goods handover (BF-07)
2.8 Session supply issue, use and return (BF-08)
A Referee or Manager requests supplies. Staff issues the available quantity. Actual use and unused returns reconcile to that allocation without a second deduction. UC-39–UC-42.

Figure 9. Session supply issue, use and return (BF-08)
2.9 Discrepancy approval and shift handover (BF-09)
Staff records a stock or payment difference. Manager, or Owner for an escalation, reviews independently. TKSC applies an approved adjustment once. UC-43–UC-46.

Figure 10. Discrepancy approval and shift handover (BF-09)
2.10 Role appointment and technical handover (BF-10)
Business authority approves the appointment. System Admin implements the approved account. A later operator change uses a separate account and preserves history. UC-29, UC-34, UC-48.

Figure 11.  Role appointment and technical handover (BF-10)

3.1. Entity Relationship Diagram
The conceptual ERD is split by business area. Repeated entity names refer to the same records. The four original views describe social play; the added views cover role accounts, separate sales and stock. Cardinality labels define required and optional links. These are business entities, not physical tables.

Each account belongs to one person and has one role. A handover names separate incoming and outgoing accounts. Sales Allocations use Bank Transactions already defined in the social-payment view, but their targets are Sales Order Items, not ticket items. Stock movements from sales handover and accepted returns point to their sales source records; no second stock item is created for the same product.

A registered participant who manages personal and sport profiles, finds and joins suitable sessions, responds to invitations, makes payments, views tickets and refunds, requests rating reviews or skill exceptions, and uses friendship, messaging and partner-preference functions.
3
Referee
An approved official who works in assigned sessions, records attendance and observed assessments, publishes supported results, decides session-specific skill exceptions, provides rating-review evidence, and requests supplies or reports their use. Referee does not make the final disputed-rating decision.
4
Staff
An operational employee who creates counter sales orders, records goods handover and stock movements, issues and receives session supplies, investigates payment exceptions, records eligible repayments, and prepares stock adjustments and shift reconciliation. Staff cannot approve their own requests or decide ratings.
5
Manager
The venue operator who manages sessions, sport assessment settings and personnel assignments, approves Staff and Referee appointments, decides disputed ratings after Referee review, and reviews operational, stock and financial exceptions within Owner-approved policy. Manager may support attendance recording.
6
Owner
The court owner who approves business policies, ticket and product prices, and Manager appointments. Owner reviews business summaries and escalated decisions and designates the incoming technical operator. This role does not grant rating-correction or unrestricted system access.
7
System Admin
The technical operator who provisions approved role accounts, maintains technical integrations, investigates incidents through necessary masked logs, retries stored verified events, and performs technical access handover. System Admin cannot decide ratings, approve business adjustments or mark unverified money as paid.
8
Google OAuth
An external supporting service that authenticates a Google identity and returns a verified sign-in result. TKSC maps that identity to a local account and applies its approved role; Google OAuth does not assign TKSC business permissions.
9
Bank / Payment Service
An external service that sends authenticated incoming transaction events for social booking or goods sales payments and receives event acknowledgements. TKSC validates and applies each transaction once. Incoming payment confirmation does not prove that an outgoing refund has been completed.
10
Cloudinary
An external supporting service that stores and manages images, receives authorized media requests, and returns media URLs, asset identifiers and operation results. It has no TKSC business role.
11
Firebase Service
An external supporting service for real-time messaging and push notifications. It exchanges authorized conversation data, message updates, notification payloads and available delivery status; TKSC retains responsibility for participant and role access checks.
12
Email Service
An external supporting service that receives recipients and message content for account verification, password recovery, booking and refund-related emails, and returns available delivery status. Email delivery does not confirm a booking, payment or repayment by itself.

II. Use Case Specifications
Normal flows state actor actions and website responses. Alternatives cover business failures and safe recovery. These checks are proposed detailed rules within FE-01–FE-18, linked to Section V.1.
1. Player onboarding
1.1. UC-04 — Complete a sport survey
Field
Specification
Actor
Primary: Player.
Description
Receive a provisional level for the selected sport.
Preconditions
The player is signed in. The selected sport has an active survey and scoring scheme.
Trigger
The player adds a sport or tries to join without a sport profile.
Normal flow
1. The website shows the selected sport and survey questions.
2. The player answers questions about experience, recent play and observable skills.
3. The website checks required answers and applies the sport's active mapping.
4. It saves the answers, scheme version and provisional score.
5. It displays the provisional label and suitable sessions for that sport.
Alternative flows
A1. Missing answers: highlight the fields; do not create a confirmed rating from incomplete data.
A2. A confirmed profile already exists: show the current result; a new survey cannot replace it.
A3. Another sport is selected: create or use its separate profile; do not convert scores.
Postconditions
A provisional sport profile is available for registration. Referees can identify it for priority observation.

Acceptance example
A confirmed badminton result stays unchanged when the player completes a pickleball survey.
2. Individual and friend registration
2.1. UC-08 — Register for a session
Field
Specification
Actor
Primary: Player. Supporting: UC-32.
Description
Hold one eligible place for the account holder.
Preconditions
The player has a sport profile. The session is open and has a configured booking deadline.
Trigger
The player selects Register on the session page.
Normal flow
1. The website displays sport, time, court, level, price and remaining places.
2. The player confirms their own registration.
3. UC-32 checks the account, sport level or exception, time conflicts and capacity.
4. The website creates one timed place hold and stores the entry conditions and price.
5. It shows the hold expiry and opens payment instructions.
Alternative flows
A1. Outside the band: explain the restriction and offer the exception-request path.
A2. Full or time conflict: create no hold; keep existing valid bookings.
A3. Repeated click: return the current registration instead of adding a second one.
A4. Another user takes the last place first: show full status and do not accept payment for a nonexistent hold.
Postconditions
One unpaid held place exists. It is not a valid ticket until UC-12 succeeds.

3.1. UC-11 — Pay for accepted registrations
Field
Specification
Actor
Primary: Player acting as payer.
Description
Pay for own or accepted friend places using the displayed bank-transfer reference.
Preconditions
Selected places are accepted, still held and payable by the current user.
Trigger
The payer proceeds to payment.
Normal flow
1. The website shows each included player, session and fixed ticket amount.
2. The payer checks the list and confirms payment for those places.
3. The website creates one unique order reference and a fixed list of order items.

3.2. UC-12 — Confirm a bank transfer
Field
Specification
Actor
Primary: Bank-transfer integration. Supporting: UC-32.
Description
Apply a verified transaction once and confirm eligible paid tickets.
Preconditions
The website receives an authenticated transaction event. The source and transaction ID can be checked.
Trigger
A payment result arrives at API-01.
Normal flow
1. The website validates the sender and required transaction fields.
2. It checks the source/transaction ID to prevent repeated application.
3. It matches receiving account, reference, currency and amount to the order.
4. It rechecks each held registration and its current entry conditions.
5. For a valid exact payment, it records allocations and confirms each eligible ticket once.
6. It updates the payer and participants with the payment/ticket details they are allowed to see.
Alternative flows
A1. Repeated event for the same transaction: return the stored result; create no new ticket or allocation.
A2. Wrong amount, missing reference, expired hold or ineligible item: record Needs review and route to UC-22; do not restore expired places automatically.
A3. Untrusted sender: reject the event and change no payment state.
A4. Write failure: allow processing to retry; do not announce a confirmed ticket before records are saved.
Postconditions
Ticket, transaction and allocation states agree. Unresolved money remains visible to Manager.
References
FE-10 | BR-03–BR-06, BR-09, BR-12–BR-14
Acceptance example
The same bank transaction delivered three times still creates only its original allocations and tickets.
3.3. UC-22 — Review an unmatched transfer
Field
Specification
Actor
Primary: Staff. Secondary: Manager for approval of business adjustments.
Description
Resolve late, partial, second-payment or unmatched-transfer cases without inventing a valid ticket.
Preconditions
A verified incoming transfer is marked Needs review.
Trigger
Staff opens the unmatched-payment list for a social or sales reference.
Normal flow
1. TKSC shows the verified transaction, intended order type and reason for review.2. Staff compares the source event with the payer and saved order items.3. Staff records evidence and a proposed match or resolution.4. Manager reviews exceptional changes; the originator cannot approve their own request.5. Before a social ticket is confirmed, the system repeats eligibility and capacity checks; a sales resolution checks stock and order state.6. It records the approved allocation once or retains the unresolved amount.
Alternative flows
A1. Partial payment: do not mark the whole order paid. Keep the outstanding amount visible.
A2. Overpayment or a second real transfer: record the surplus separately; do not create extra seats.

5. Background Job Inventory
5.1. JOB-01 — Expire unpaid place holds and invitations
Field
Requirement
Trigger
Scheduled processing of configured expiry times.
Processing
1. Find pending invitations and held registrations whose expiry has passed.
2. Recheck payment state in the same protected update before expiring a hold.
3. Expire only unpaid items, release their places and close unpaid payment items.
4. Keep confirmed tickets and other friends' paid places unchanged.
Failure behaviour
On error, keep a record for retry. Registration checks also reject expired holds, so a delayed job cannot extend their rights.
5.2. JOB-02 — Check future tickets after a published rating change
Field
Requirement
Trigger
A saved publication or correction creates a job for that player, sport and result version.
Processing
1. Reload the latest published sport profile before evaluating a ticket.
2. Find future paid tickets in the affected sport and check current bands/exceptions.
3. For an ineligible ticket, perform UC-21 once and store the outcome.
4. Retain eligible tickets and save completion progress for retries.
Failure behaviour
Retry from recorded progress. Do not duplicate an invalidation or refund. Do not use an older queued rating after a newer correction is published.
5.3. JOB-03 — Retry verified payment processing
Field
Requirement
Trigger
A trusted event was stored but could not finish processing.
Processing
1. Load the trusted event and its saved processing identity.2. Resolve its unique original order reference and route social payments to UC-12 or sales payments to UC-37.3. Recheck the relevant ticket or stock reservation using current records.4. Reuse existing allocations and save one final result or a visible review case. Never switch order type, bypass conditions or apply the bank amount twice.
Failure behaviour
Keep business exceptions visible to Staff and Manager; System Admin sees masked processing errors. A technical retry does not bypass an expired hold, skill rule or full session.
5.4. JOB-04 — Release expired unpaid sales reservations
Field
Requirement
Trigger
A configured unpaid Sales Order expires.
Processing
1. Find expired unpaid orders.2. Recheck the recorded payment and current reservation in the protected update.3. Release only that order’s reserved stock; keep paid reservations and unrelated orders.4. Mark the unpaid order expired and retain its reference for later transaction review.
Failure behaviour
Retry from the saved state without releasing the same reservation twice. A late bank event stays visible for Staff/Manager review; expiry never hides incoming money.
6. External API Inventory
6.1. Receive bank-transfer events (API-01)
Field
Specification
Purpose
Provide authenticated transaction results to UC-12 for social orders or UC-37 for goods orders, based on a unique reference.
Caller
The approved bank-transfer integration; not the player's browser.
Proposed endpoint
POST /api/integrations/bank-transfers/events
Authentication
Verify origin and message integrity using the selected integration's supported authentication. Reject unauthenticated requests before changing transaction data.
Normalized input
Source ID; external transaction ID; receiving account identity; transfer reference; incoming amount; currency; bank transaction time. Retain available evidence for reconciliation.
Success response
Acknowledge the saved event and return a stable processing status. A receipt acknowledgement is not a ticket confirmation.
Duplicate event
Return the recorded result for the same source/transaction ID without applying money again.
Invalid or unmatched input
Reject untrusted or invalid events. Retain authenticated unmatched amounts for Staff investigation and Manager review. No order type or payer is guessed.

Acknowledge only a saved event. JOB-03 resumes it with the same identity and original order type. A retry cannot duplicate a social/sales allocation, oversell seats/stock or bypass approval.
Refund processing is specified as a business workflow and verified repayment record in UC-21. An incoming-transfer connection is not permission or proof that outgoing money has been sent.

Create independent references for social and sales orders. A payment targets one order in this release. Shared bank events and allocations must not apply the same money twice.
UC-12, UC-22, UC-37, API-01
BR-35
Payment is not delivery
A paid Sales Order is not delivered until Staff records physical handover. A repeated handover creates no second goods deduction.
UC-37, UC-38
BR-36
Protected stock availability
Reserve goods for a payable sales order using current available stock. Available = on hand - active sales reservations - outstanding session allocations. Do not allow a negative result.
UC-36, UC-38, UC-41
BR-37
One stock event once
A receipt, sale, use, return or approved adjustment has a unique source-event identity. Repeating it returns its stored result; it does not change stock twice.
UC-38, UC-39, UC-41–UC-44
BR-38
Allocation is not consumption
Issuing session supplies moves quantity to the session allocation. Use reduces both that allocation and on hand; an unused return only releases the allocation. Use plus returns cannot exceed issued quantity.
UC-41, UC-42
BR-39
Review stock differences
Staff records count differences with evidence. Only an approved adjustment changes the balance. Do not silently replace expected stock with the entered count.
UC-43, UC-44
BR-40
Separate reviewer
Staff prepares exceptional money or stock changes. Manager decides within approved authority; Owner handles the escalated business decision. The originator cannot approve their own request.
UC-22, UC-43–UC-46, UC-50
BR-41
No refund cross-effects
A rating-change refund affects only its social ticket allocation. It does not cancel goods purchases. A goods refund does not cancel a social booking.
UC-21, UC-50
BR-42
Return is not refund
Do not add returned goods to sellable stock until physical receipt and condition are recorded under an approved decision. A payout does not itself restock an item.
UC-50
BR-43
Approved prices
Owner approves ticket/product prices and their effective version. Staff cannot change the price of a paid order or apply an unapproved discount. Manager publishes social sessions within those rules.
UC-26, UC-33, UC-36
BR-44
Traceable shift results
Show social sales, goods sales, verified refunds and unresolved money separately. A reconciliation approval does not erase the original transactions or pending items.
UC-35, UC-45
BR-45
Technical access is not business authority
System Admin implements approved access and technical settings, views only required masked logs and retries saved events. This role cannot approve business adjustments or turn unverified money into a payment.
UC-29, UC-47, UC-48
BR-46
Separate operator at handover
Đức supports the venue through TK.admin initially. A later incoming administrator receives their own TK.admin.<identifier> account. Keep TK.manager separate; revoke former support access without reassigning its history.
UC-48
BR-47
Role-safe external sign-in
A verified Google identity maps to a specific local account and its one role. External sign-in never creates Owner, Manager, Staff, Referee or System Admin rights through self-registration.
UC-01, UC-02, UC-49
BR-48
No assumed cash or return policy
This release records bank transfers. A goods return, cancellation or exceptional refund requires the relevant approved business decision; no default cash policy, fee, deposit or deadline is invented.
UC-36, UC-44, UC-50
BR-49
Unpaid stock expiry
Expired unpaid Sales Orders release only their own reservations after payment state is rechecked. Late transfers enter review rather than forcing a goods handover without stock.
UC-36, UC-37, JOB-04
BR-50
History cannot hide an approval change
Changing the approved amount, recipient, quantity or target account invalidates the earlier decision for that action. Record and review the changed request before applying it.
